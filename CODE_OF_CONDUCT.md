# OpenBook Code of Conduct

## Our Pledge

We are committed to making the OpenBook project welcoming, safe, equitable, and constructive for everyone who participates in good faith. We value respectful collaboration and the dignity, rights, and contributions of every participant.

This Code of Conduct applies without regard to characteristics including race, ethnicity, caste, color, age, disability, neurodiversity, sex or gender, gender identity or expression, sexual orientation, language, philosophy or religion, national or social origin, socio-economic position, education, or other status.

## Expected Behavior

Participants are expected to:

- Treat others with respect, professionalism, and consideration.
- Engage kindly and honestly, including when disagreeing.
- Respect different viewpoints, experiences, cultures, and communication styles.
- Take responsibility for their actions and contributions.
- Give and receive constructive feedback in good faith.
- Respect the purpose of OpenBook, its technical governance, and its community spaces.
- Help repair harm when their words or actions have caused harm.

## Unacceptable Behavior

The following behavior is not acceptable in OpenBook project spaces or when representing the project:

- Harassment, intimidation, threats, or targeted abuse.
- Insulting, demeaning, or personally attacking individuals or groups.
- Discrimination or stereotyping based on identity or protected or immutable characteristics.
- Unwelcome sexualized or otherwise inappropriate conduct.
- Sharing, seeking, or using another person's private or confidential information without permission.
- Causing, encouraging, or threatening violence or other harm.
- Impersonation or deliberate evasion of project enforcement.
- Failure to credit sources for contributed material where attribution is required.
- Promotional or commercial material that is inconsistent with the norms of the project spaces.
- Communication that irresponsibly promotes, facilitates, or normalizes prohibited conduct.
- Other conduct that materially threatens the safety or well-being of the project community.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies to contributors, maintainers, reviewers, issue and pull-request participants, project representatives, and other participants in official OpenBook community spaces. It also applies when someone is officially representing OpenBook in public or other spaces, including through official project accounts, email addresses, or designated project events.

This Code of Conduct governs participant conduct. It does not replace or override OpenBook's technical governance, architecture decisions, contribution requirements, licensing terms, pull-request process, CI requirements, or branch protection.

## Reporting an Issue

If you believe this Code of Conduct has been violated, report the incident privately to **contact@sanmitratech.in**. Do not use public GitHub Issues or pull requests to report sensitive Code of Conduct incidents.

Where reasonably possible, include:

- What happened and why you believe it violates this Code of Conduct.
- The approximate date and time of the incident.
- The people involved and any relevant witnesses.
- Links, messages, or other evidence that can safely be provided.
- A preferred method for follow-up, if applicable.

Please provide only information that is reasonably safe and available to you. Good-faith reports are welcomed and will not be treated adversely merely because an investigation does not substantiate a violation.

## Confidentiality and Investigation

Reports will be handled privately and on a need-to-know basis. Reporter identity and investigation materials will not be unnecessarily disclosed or discussed publicly. Absolute confidentiality cannot be guaranteed where disclosure is legally required or necessary to address an immediate safety threat.

The project will review reports fairly and proportionately, considering relevant messages, records, and witness information where appropriate. Enforcement actions are handled privately with the involved parties, although a general community communication may be appropriate when necessary and safe and does not unnecessarily disclose personal information.

## Enforcement Authority

SanMitra Tech Solutions is the project steward under ADR-0025 and has ultimate stewardship responsibility for Code of Conduct enforcement. Day-to-day enforcement may be carried out by designated maintainers acting within the project's governance framework.

A maintainer who is the subject of a report must not be the sole decision-maker for that report. The matter must be escalated to another authorized maintainer or to the project steward as appropriate.

All enforcement decisions remain subject to the project's governance boundaries. Code of Conduct enforcement does not authorize bypassing pull-request review, CI, branch protection, DCO requirements, or the ADR process.

## Addressing and Repairing Harm

Responses will be proportionate to the circumstances and impact of the conduct. Depending on severity and context, the project may use one or more of the following measures:

1. **Warning** — a private written warning and clarification of expected behavior.
2. **Temporarily limited participation** — a time-limited restriction on specified project interactions or spaces.
3. **Temporary suspension** — temporary removal of participation rights, with conditions for return where appropriate.
4. **Permanent removal** — removal of access to project/community spaces when necessary to protect the community, particularly after serious or repeated violations.

Serious violations may warrant skipping lower levels of the response ladder. The project may use its judgment to select an appropriate response based on the facts and safety of the community.

## Appeals and Review

A participant may request a limited review of an enforcement decision when there is materially new information, a significant procedural error, a credible conflict of interest, or a credible concern that the decision was inconsistent with this Code of Conduct.

An appeal is not a mechanism for reopening technical disagreements about code, architecture, pull requests, or project direction. Where the original decision involved a maintainer, review should be handled by another authorized maintainer or the project steward.

## Relationship to Project Governance

This Code of Conduct complements, and does not amend, the OpenBook governance framework. In particular:

- DCO 1.1 remains the contribution sign-off mechanism.
- Apache-2.0 remains the project's governing software license.
- ADR-0025 remains the stewardship and maintainer governance decision.
- Architecture and technical decisions continue to follow the ADR process.
- Repository changes continue to require the normal pull-request, CI, and branch-protection controls.
