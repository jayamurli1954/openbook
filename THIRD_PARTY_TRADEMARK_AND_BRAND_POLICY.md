# OpenBook Studio — Third-Party Trademark & Brand-Asset Policy

**Status:** Foundation Governance Policy  
**Project:** OpenBook Studio  
**Maintainer / Project Steward:** SanMitra Tech Solutions  
**Governing Context:** Works in conjunction with `LICENSING_POLICY.md` and `THIRD_PARTY_REFERENCE_POLICY.md`.

---

## 1. Purpose & Core Philosophy

OpenBook is committed to strict respect for third-party intellectual property, trademarks, trade dress, and brand identities. While OpenBook leverages world-class open-source engines, frameworks, and libraries (e.g., Tauri, Rust, Typst, EPUBCheck, React, Tiptap), **open-source software copyright licenses (MIT, Apache 2.0, MPL, etc.) do NOT grant trademark licenses or rights to use upstream logos, marks, or branding.**

OpenBook maintains complete brand sovereignty: OpenBook must never present third-party brand assets as its own, nor create false impressions of affiliation, sponsorship, certification, or endorsement.

---

## 2. Universal Scope of Enforcement

This policy applies without exception across:
1. **Application Shell & UI:** Application desktop icons, window chrome, splash screens, dialogs, about boxes, empty states, toolbars, and system tray icons.
2. **Binary Distributions & Bundles:** Installers (MSI, DMG, DEB, AppImage), app stores (Windows Store, Mac App Store), and release tarballs.
3. **Repository Code & Scaffolding:** Source files, assets directories, test fixtures, boilerplates, and generator output.
4. **Documentation & Media:** READMEs, architectural docs, website pages, tutorial videos, promotional graphics, and screenshots.
5. **Sample Content & Templates:** Shipped demo books, cover templates, typography presets, and export stylesheets.

---

## 3. Core Policy Rules

### Rule 1: No Third-Party Branding as OpenBook Branding
Third-party logos, icons, mascots, or trade dress must **never** be used as the application icon, window icon, splash graphic, repository avatar, or branding for OpenBook Studio or any of its constituent packages. OpenBook branding must be owned by, created for, or otherwise appropriately licensed to OpenBook, with provenance documented.

### Rule 2: Strict Ban on Unchecked Scaffolding Artifacts (The Tauri Lesson)
When utilizing CLI scaffolding tools or project generators (e.g., `create-tauri-app`, `npm init`, `cargo new`), any default placeholder icons, vendor logos, or template branding generated by the tool **must be quarantined or replaced before the affected change is merged to `main`**. Upstream scaffolding output must never be presumed legally safe for distribution.

### Rule 3: Nominative Use & Mandatory Ownership Attribution
Legitimate references to third-party tools, formats, or platforms (e.g., "Export to EPUB", "Compiled via Typst", "Built with Tauri", "Validated with EPUBCheck") must:
- Be strictly nominative and factual (used solely to identify compatibility, technology stack, or interoperability).
- Avoid using third-party logos where plain text suffices.
- Clearly identify and attribute the relevant trademark owner in legal notices where appropriate and where required by the applicable trademark policy, license, or other governing terms.

### Rule 4: Zero Implication of Endorsement or Affiliation
No wording, visual juxtaposition, or layout may state or imply that OpenBook is officially endorsed, sponsored, certified, authored by, or partnered with any third-party project or company unless an explicit, written authorization or agreement exists.

### Rule 5: Prohibition of Purely Decorative Brand Marks
Third-party logos must not be embedded merely for decoration, page balance, or cosmetic embellishment in documentation, UI buttons, table cells, or headers. Use plain, semantic text or standard generic utility icons.

### Rule 6: Pre-Adoption Provenance & Trademark Clearance
Confirm that the intended use is permitted under the applicable license, trademark policy, published brand guidelines, applicable law, or other documented authorization. Where permission is required, obtain and document that permission. If rights, permissible use, or provenance remain uncertain, the asset MUST NOT be adopted until reviewed.

### Rule 7: Precautionary Quarantining
If an existing repository asset is discovered to contain third-party trademarked material without affirmative clearance:
1. It must be flagged immediately for provenance review.
2. Pending releases containing the asset must be halted. Existing releases must be assessed promptly and, where appropriate, withdrawn, replaced, or otherwise remediated through the project's release process.
3. The asset must be replaced with an appropriately owned or licensed OpenBook asset or removed entirely in a dedicated clean-up PR.

---

## 4. Compliance Checklist for Contributors & Reviewers

Every PR introducing or altering visual assets, templates, icons, or third-party mentions must verify:
- [ ] Are all app icons, splash screens, and UI elements owned by, created for, or appropriately licensed to OpenBook?
- [ ] Did generator/scaffold scripts introduce upstream vendor logos into `icons/` or `assets/`?
- [ ] Are third-party tools cited in plain text rather than protected logos?
- [ ] Is there zero suggestion of official endorsement or partnership?
- [ ] Are trademark ownership notices preserved in `NOTICE` / `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` where required?
